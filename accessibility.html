Accessibility

Accessibility Statement

Last updated 17 August 2026. Hollis & Vane Ltd., 214 Lafayette Street, Suite 4, New York, NY 10012, United States.

Hollis & Vane is committed to making this site usable by as many people as possible, including people using screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, screen magnification, speech input and reduced-motion settings. This statement describes where we are, honestly, including what still needs work.

Conformance status

We target the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA. We believe this site substantially conforms to that standard. "Substantially" is doing real work in that sentence: it means we have tested the templates and found no known Level A or AA failures, not that we have audited every page against every criterion with an external auditor. When we have completed an independent audit we will say so here and publish the date.

What is built in

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmarks: a single main region, header, navigation and footer, with one h1 per page and headings in order.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable element on every page jumps past the navigation.
  • Keyboard access. Every interactive element, including the mobile menu, filters, accordions, the product gallery, forms and the cookie banner, is reachable and operable by keyboard alone. The mobile menu traps focus while open and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • Visible focus. Focus is indicated with a solid outline offset from the element, on every control, in every state. We have not removed focus rings anywhere.
  • Contrast. Body text is pure black on white. Secondary text is black at 68% opacity, which measures approximately 6.6:1 against white, above the 4.5:1 requirement. The warm grey in our palette is used only for large text, hairline rules and decorative marks, never for body copy, because at small sizes it would not meet the ratio.
  • Images. Every content image carries descriptive alternative text stating what is shown and how the garment is worn. Decorative thumbnails inside labelled controls carry empty alt attributes so screen readers do not announce them twice.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label, not a placeholder acting as one. Errors appear inline beneath the field they belong to, are announced politely to screen readers, and describe how to fix the problem rather than simply stating that something is wrong.
  • Motion. The site respects prefers-reduced-motion. Under that setting all transitions collapse to instant, scroll behaviour becomes immediate, the cursor dot is disabled and the native pointer returns, and the product image cross-fade is switched off.
  • Zoom and reflow. Content reflows to a single readable column at 320 pixels wide and at 400% zoom without horizontal scrolling. Text can be resized to 200% without loss of function. We do not disable pinch zoom.
  • No traps. There are no interstitials on entry, no auto-playing audio or video, no content that flashes, and no time limits on any interaction.

Known limitations

Three, stated plainly rather than buried.

  • The custom cursor. On desktop devices with a mouse we replace the pointer with a small dot. It is disabled automatically under reduced-motion settings and on touch devices, and it never affects keyboard use, but we know a non-standard cursor is unhelpful for some people. If you want it gone permanently, enabling reduced motion in your operating system turns it off.
  • Data tables. The size and shipping tables scroll horizontally on narrow screens. They have proper row and column headers and a caption, so they are readable with a screen reader, but horizontal scrolling on a phone is awkward for anyone with limited dexterity.
  • Third-party advertising. Advertising slots may be filled by content we do not author and cannot make accessible ourselves. Rejecting advertising cookies removes them, and no advertising appears on legal pages or the error page.

Compatibility

The site is tested with current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on macOS, Windows, iOS and Android, and with VoiceOver on macOS and iOS and NVDA on Windows. It should work with other assistive technology, but we have not tested every combination. It requires JavaScript for the bag, the filters and the accordions; without JavaScript, every page still renders in full, every link works and all content is readable.

Alternatives to using the site

If any part of this site prevents you from ordering, we will take the order for you. Call +1 (212) 555-0148, Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00 ET, or email clientcare@hollisandvane.com. We can read out measurements, describe garments in detail, take payment over the phone and arrange delivery, at exactly the same prices. This is not a lesser service and there is no surcharge.

Reporting a barrier

If you meet something on this site that does not work for you, please tell us. Email accessibility@hollisandvane.com or call the number above. Tell us the page, what you were trying to do, and the browser and assistive technology you were using if you know them.

We acknowledge every report within two business days and give you a plan and a target date within ten. If we cannot fix something quickly, we will tell you what the workaround is in the meantime.

Formal complaints

If our response is not adequate, write to Hollis & Vane Ltd., Attn: Accessibility, 214 Lafayette Street, Suite 4, New York, NY 10012, and a director will review it personally. You may also contact the US Department of Justice regarding Americans with Disabilities Act concerns.
