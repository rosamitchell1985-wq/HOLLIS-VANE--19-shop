The house

Eleven pieces, and a rule

Hollis & Vane was founded in 2016 by Marguerite Hollis and Teodor Vane, a pattern cutter and a production manager who had spent a decade watching good cloth get discounted into landfill. The label started with eleven pieces and one rule that has not changed since.

The rule

Nothing enters the collection unless it replaces something the wearer already owns. Not adds to. Replaces. It sounds like marketing until you try to design against it, at which point it kills roughly four ideas out of five.

Ten years in, the capsule holds twenty-eight styles. It has held between twenty-four and thirty for six years. Pieces leave when a mill retires a cloth, when a better construction becomes possible, or when customers tell us for two years running that a trouser is cut too narrow to wear over boots. Nothing leaves because a season ended, because our seasons do not end.

What we are not

We are not a sustainable brand, because we sell clothing and selling clothing is not sustainable. We are a small brand that makes fewer things, tells you exactly what they are made of, repairs them for free, and does not run sales that train you to wait. The difference matters and we would rather state it plainly than borrow a word we have not earned. Our full materials and impact position is on Sustainability & Materials.

We are also not a luxury house. We have no heritage, no atelier in Paris, no archive. What we have is a small production run with three factories we visit twice a year and a price list with one margin in it.

Who makes it

Three factories make almost everything we sell. We name them because a brand that will not name its factories is usually hiding either the conditions or the fact that it does not know.

  • Confeccoes Aveleda, Porto, Portugal. Outerwear, shirting and trousers. Family owned, 62 employees, working with us since 2017. Audited annually under amfori BSCI.
  • Glenlaurie Knitwear, Dumfries, Scotland. All knitwear. 41 employees, hand-linking done on site. Working with us since 2018.
  • Taller Ferrer, Igualada, Spain. Leather goods, including the made-to-order holdall. Eleven leatherworkers. Working with us since 2021.

Our denim is sewn in Los Angeles by a 24-person shop we have used since 2022, from cloth woven in Okayama. Our silk is woven and dyed in Hangzhou at an OEKO-TEX certified mill, which is the part of our supply chain we have the least visibility into and we say so rather than leaving it off the list.

Cloth resting before cutting. Wool that is cut straight off the roll twists within a year.

Why we never discount

A sale is a message. It says the price you paid last month was wrong, and that the price you see today may be wrong too, so wait. Once a customer learns to wait, the brand has to build the discount into the original price, and everyone ends up paying for the theatre.

We do not run seasonal sales, Black Friday, flash sales or private codes. The only price reductions we ever apply are on genuinely imperfect garments, which we sell as seconds from the studio with the fault described, and on ex-photography samples once a year. Both are announced by email and both sell in a morning.

The trade for you is a lower everyday price. The trade for us is that we have to get the buy right, because there is no discount lever to pull in January.

What we do with what is left

Overproduction is the failure state of this business. In 2025 we ended the year with 312 units of unsold stock across the collection, about 3.1% of what we made. Because the capsule is seasonless, almost all of it simply carried into 2026 and sold at full price.

Genuinely unsellable stock, meaning faulty or sample garments, goes three ways: seconds sales from the studio, donation to two New York shelters we have supported since 2019, and, for anything that cannot be worn, fibre recycling through a Massachusetts processor. We have never sent finished garments to landfill and we have never destroyed stock to protect price. If that ever changes we will say so here.

For wholesale and stockist enquiries, the form and the terms are on Stockists. For press, write to press@hollisandvane.com and we will send lookbook files and product samples in the size you need.
