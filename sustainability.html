Materials

What we do not know

Three gaps, stated plainly.

  • Silk. We can verify the mill and its chemical management. We cannot yet verify conditions at the farms supplying that mill. We have asked twice and been given aggregate assurances rather than audit access.
  • Denim cotton. Our Okayama mill buys conventional cotton on the open market. Its origin varies by lot and we cannot trace it to farm. We keep this piece in the collection because the cloth is exceptional and the alternative is a certified fabric that does not exist at this weight, but we are not going to pretend the gap is closed.
  • Scope 3 emissions. We measure our own operations and freight. We do not have verified emissions data from our mills. Any total we published would be an estimate wearing a suit, so we do not publish one.

What we are reasonably confident about

All three main factories are audited annually, two under amfori BSCI and one under SMETA, and we hold the reports. We visit each of them at least twice a year. Every worker making our garments is employed directly by the factory rather than through a labour agency, which we check against payroll during visits.

Repair, which is the real programme

The single most useful thing we do for the life of a garment is fix it rather than replace it. Repairs are free for the life of the piece on anything we made, with no receipt required and no time limit.

  • Reknitting moth holes and snags in knitwear.
  • Reweaving small damage in woven wool.
  • Restitching, edge refinishing and zip replacement on leather goods, charged at cost because the workshop is in Spain.
  • Rebuttoning, hemming, sleeve shortening and belt re-punching.

In 2025 we repaired 214 garments. The oldest was a 2017 overcoat that came back for a reweave at the cuff and a new pair of buttons. Send anything to the studio address in the footer with a note, or start the process through client care.

2025 in numbers

  • 10,140 units produced across 28 styles.
  • 312 units unsold at year end, 3.1%, carried into 2026 at full price.
  • 214 garments repaired free of charge.
  • Zero finished garments sent to landfill.
  • Zero stock destroyed to protect price.
  • 6.2% of orders returned, of which 61% were exchanges rather than refunds.

These are our own figures, unaudited, and we publish them because a brand that only publishes the flattering ones is not telling you much.

Packaging and freight

Orders ship in an uncoated recycled cardboard box with paper tape and a paper packing note. No plastic polybags leave our warehouse, and garments arrive from our factories in reusable cotton bags that we send back on the next inbound shipment.

Everything travels by sea from Europe. We use air freight only when a factory has missed a date and a customer is waiting on a made-to-order piece, which happened four times in 2025.
