The Mono Overcoat

$1,290

The coat the collection is built around. Cut from a double-face wool woven in Biella, Italy, so there is no lining to twist or tear, and closed with two horn buttons set low on the body. It falls to mid-calf on most wearers and holds a straight line even when the wind is against it.

Fabric

  • 80% virgin wool, 20% alpaca, double-face construction, 780 g per linear metre
  • Woven by Lanificio Fratelli Piacenza, Biella, Italy
  • Hand-finished bound edges, no fusible interlining anywhere in the garment
  • Horn buttons from a British button house, sourced as a byproduct of the food industry

Fit

  • Relaxed straight body with a deliberate drop shoulder
  • Falls to mid-calf: 48 in centre back on size M
  • Room to layer a heavy knit underneath without pulling at the chest
  • Between sizes, take the smaller one

Care

  • Dry clean sparingly, once or twice a season at most
  • Brush along the nap with a soft garment brush after each wear
  • Hang on a wide wooden hanger; wire hangers deform the shoulder
  • Free reweaving of moth damage or snags at our New York studio, for life

Sizing

  • XS to XXL, equivalent to US 0 through US 18
  • Model is 5 ft 11 in and wears a size S
  • Second model is 6 ft 2 in and wears a size L
  • Full chest, waist and sleeve measurements in inches are in the Size Guide

The Boxy Wool Blazer

$845

An unstructured blazer with no shoulder pad and no chest canvas, which is why it packs flat and why it reads as a soft jacket rather than tailoring. Three patch pockets, one interior. It is the piece customers most often tell us they wear five days a week.

Fabric

  • 100% virgin wool tropical suiting, 310 g per square metre
  • Milled in Huddersfield, England
  • Half lined in cupro derived from cotton linter
  • Corozo nut buttons, undyed

Fit

  • Boxy through the body with a squared hem
  • Hits at the high hip: 27 in centre back on size M
  • Sleeve finishes at the wrist bone, intended to be worn unrolled
  • Sits cleanly over a knit tee or a fine-gauge sweater

Care

  • Dry clean only
  • Steam rather than press to keep the soft shoulder
  • Rest the jacket a day between wears so the wool recovers
  • Rebuttoning and lining repairs at cost, for life

Sizing

  • XS to XL, equivalent to US 0 through US 14
  • Model is 5 ft 9 in and wears a size M
  • Take your usual jacket size; the cut already allows layering
  • Measurements in inches in the Size Guide

The Long Trench

$980

A trench stripped of its military hardware. No epaulettes, no storm flap, no D-rings. What remains is a tightly woven cotton gabardine, a self belt, and a hem that clears the ankle by two inches so it does not drag on wet pavement.

Fabric

  • 100% long-staple cotton gabardine, densely woven and rain resistant without coating
  • Woven in Osaka, Japan
  • Undyed horn buckle on the self belt
  • No PFAS-based water repellent is used on this garment

Fit

  • Straight and slightly A-line from the shoulder
  • Falls to the lower calf: 50 in centre back on size M
  • Belted or open, both intended
  • Cut to close comfortably over a blazer

Care

  • Machine wash cold on a gentle cycle, or dry clean
  • Line dry away from direct heat; the gabardine softens with each wash
  • Do not tumble dry
  • Water resistance improves as the cotton fibres swell with washing

Sizing

  • XS to XL, equivalent to US 0 through US 14
  • Model is 5 ft 10 in and wears a size S
  • Sleeve runs long by design; we shorten free of charge on request
  • Measurements in inches in the Size Guide

The Quilted Liner Jacket

$640

Designed to be worn two ways: on its own in the shoulder seasons, or buttoned inside The Mono Overcoat and The Long Trench as a liner. The quilting is channel stitched rather than boxed, which keeps the silhouette flat instead of puffed.

Fabric

  • Shell and lining in 100% recycled polyamide from post-industrial waste
  • Fill is 100% recycled polyester wadding, 120 g per square metre
  • Bluesign approved shell fabric
  • Buttons match the liner posts inside our coats

Fit

  • Slim through the body so it disappears under outerwear
  • Hits at the hip: 26 in centre back on size M

Care

  • Machine wash cold, gentle cycle, with the buttons closed
  • Tumble dry low to redistribute the fill
  • Do not iron the quilted panels
  • Small tears can be patched at our studio at cost

Sizing

  • S to XXL, equivalent to US 4 through US 18
  • Model is 6 ft 0 in and wears a size M
  • Take your usual size

The Cropped Field Coat

$720

A short coat with four bellows pockets, cut in a waxed-feel cotton canvas that has not been waxed at all. The finish comes from the weave and a mechanical calendering step, so the coat can go through a washing machine like any other cotton garment.

Fabric

  • 100% organic cotton canvas, 420 g per square metre, GOTS certified
  • Woven and finished in Porto, Portugal
  • Cotton twill tape reinforcement at every stress point
  • Metal snaps, nickel free

Fit

  • Boxy and short, sits at the top of the hip
  • Length: 25 in centre back on size M
  • Deliberately wide through the sleeve for layering over knitwear
  • Works over the Fisherman Sweater without pulling

Care

  • Machine wash cold, gentle cycle
  • Line dry; the canvas stiffens then softens again with wear
  • Warm iron if you want the pockets to sit flat
  • Snap replacement free of charge, for life

Sizing

  • XS to XL, equivalent to US 0 through US 14
  • Model is 5 ft 8 in and wears a size M for a looser fall
  • Size down if you prefer it close to the body

The Double-Face Robe Coat

$1,480

The most expensive thing we make, and the one that takes the longest. Two faces of cashmere-blend cloth are bonded and then separated by hand at every edge so the seams can be closed invisibly. A single coat takes a finisher most of a working day.

Fabric

  • 70% virgin wool, 30% cashmere, double-face, 690 g per linear metre
  • Cloth woven in Biella, Italy; coat made in Porto, Portugal
  • Every edge hand-separated and slip stitched closed
  • No lining, no interfacing, no fusing

Fit

  • Wrap silhouette with a self belt and no buttons
  • Falls below the knee: 46 in centre back on size M
  • Generous through the body, intended to be worn over a full outfit
  • The belt sets the waist wherever you want it

Care

  • Dry clean only, and rarely
  • Store folded over a padded hanger rather than hung by the shoulders
  • Brush after wear; cashmere sheds surface dirt easily
  • Hand re-stitching of edges at our studio, at cost, for life

Sizing

  • XS to L, equivalent to US 0 through US 12
  • Model is 5 ft 11 in and wears a size XS
  • Runs generous; most people take one size below their usual coat size
  • Measurements in inches in the Size Guide

The Shearling Car Coat

$1,950  /  Made to order, 9 weeks

Cut only after you order it. Shearling is bought by the hide and no two hides are identical, so we match panels per coat rather than per production run. Nine weeks from order to doorstep, and we email you a photograph of your hides before cutting begins.

Fabric

  • Spanish merino shearling, entrada tannery, chrome free
  • Hides are a byproduct of the food industry and fully traceable to farm
  • Wool side worn in, suede side worn out
  • Horn buttons, hand sewn

Fit

  • Straight car-coat length, generous through the shoulder
  • Length: 34 in centre back on size M
  • Cut to close over a knit but not over a blazer

Care

  • Specialist shearling cleaning only, and only when genuinely needed
  • Shake out and air rather than clean after most wears
  • Never machine wash, never tumble dry, never store in plastic
  • Reconditioning and reshaping through our partner workshop, at cost

Sizing

  • S to XL, equivalent to US 4 through US 14
  • Model is 6 ft 0 in and wears a size M

Why the coats cost what they cost

A double-face wool from Biella costs us roughly $61 per linear metre and a coat takes 3.4 metres. Cutting, hand-finishing and pressing take a Portuguese atelier close to five hours. Add trims, freight, duty and the failure rate on a cloth that cannot be re-cut, and the landed cost of The Mono Overcoat is a little over $430.

A department store would take that to roughly $2,400 through wholesale and retail margin. We sell it at $1,290 because there is only one margin in the chain. Read the full arithmetic in Our Story.
